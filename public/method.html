How it works

From official statistics to a forecast you can use

CiO Costs combines official construction price statistics with the things that move construction costs week to week, and turns them into a chance, an expected change and a likely range for each country.

1What goes in

Official construction price indices where they exist (Uganda, Kenya and South Africa in full; latest readings for Ghana, Zimbabwe, Senegal and Mauritius), world oil prices, exchange rates for 18 currencies, rainfall, and published tax changes such as fuel and cement duties.

2How it is forecast

Where a country publishes a detailed index, each material is forecast from the drivers that move it — oil for diesel, the currency for imported steel, rainfall for sand — and added back up the way the statistics office does. Elsewhere, an import-cost indicator tracks the share of costs that moves with oil and the exchange rate.

3How it is checked

Every method is tested on years of past data, as if it had been run at the time, and kept only if it beats simple rules of thumb. In Uganda the forecasts have been about 11–14% more accurate than a simple trend, and the likely ranges are set so that 8 in 10 past outcomes fell inside.

4How often it updates

Oil and exchange rates are refreshed every few hours and the forecasts with them. Official data is added within a day of publication. A written note for each country comes out every Monday.

Reading the numbers

  • Chance of a rise: how likely prices are to be higher by the month you pick.
  • Expected change: the most likely move, measured from the latest official reading (or from today for import-cost indicators).
  • Likely range: where the change should land 8 times in 10.

Limits

  • Forecasts are estimates, not certainties. Always use the range.
  • Import-cost indicators see oil and currency effects only, not local materials or labour.
  • Where official statistics are published late, the forecast bridges the gap with the latest market data.
  • Nothing here is financial or contractual advice.
